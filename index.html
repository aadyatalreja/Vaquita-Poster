The Species

WHAT IS THE
VAQUITA?

The Vaquita (Phocoena sinus) — meaning "little cow" in Spanish — is the smallest and rarest cetacean on Earth. Found exclusively in the shallow, murky waters of the northern Gulf of California, Mexico, it is critically endangered due to bycatch in illegal fishing nets. Once numbering in the thousands, fewer than 10 individuals are believed to survive today.

Marine Habitat

Shallow coastal lagoons and tidal channels, 0–50 m depth

Critically Endangered

IUCN Red List — Critically Endangered since 1996, population collapsing

Gulf of California

Unique endemic range — found nowhere else on the planet

~1.5 m Body Length

Smallest porpoise in the world — about the size of a 10-year-old child

Shy & Elusive

Rarely seen — typically flees from boats; prefers cloudy, murky water

Coastal Endemic

Prefers warm, shallow waters with rich fish and squid populations

For Fishermen · Identificación

HOW TO
IDENTIFY
A VAQUITA

If you are fishing in the northern Gulf of California, you may encounter a Vaquita. Knowing how to recognise it is essential — both to protect this critically endangered animal and to comply with conservation law. Here are the key features to look for.

  • Dark rings around the eyes
    Distinctive black eye-patches — like spectacles — clearly visible at close range
  • Dark lip patches
    Curved dark markings on the lips giving the appearance of a faint smile
  • Light grey body, white underside
    Overall pale grey colouring on the back, fading to white on the belly
  • Short, blunt snout (no beak)
    Unlike dolphins, the Vaquita has a rounded head with no elongated rostrum
  • Large triangular dorsal fin
    Relatively tall dorsal fin for its small body size — helps distinguish it from dolphins
  • Compact, stocky body (~1.5 m)
    Much smaller than a bottlenose dolphin — roughly the size of a large dog
  • Quick, shy surface behaviour
    Briefly surfaces to breathe and quickly dives — rarely jumps or approaches boats

If you spot a Vaquita: Stop all net activity immediately. Give the animal ample space to move freely. Do not pursue or approach it. Report the sighting to CONAPESCA or SEMARNAT conservation authorities at once. Your observation could be one of the most important wildlife sightings in the world.

The Crisis

THE SILENT
TRAP

The Vaquita does not die from disease or natural predation. It drowns — tangled silently in illegal gillnets set to catch the totoaba fish, whose swim bladder is smuggled illegally to China for traditional medicine. Every net left in Vaquita habitat is a potential death sentence.

Fewer than 19 remain — illegal totoaba poaching reaches peak
2022–2024
Estimated 6–10 surviving individuals — each sighting is a miracle

Illegal Gillnet Bycatch

Transparent monofilament nets set for totoaba fish are invisible underwater. Vaquitas swim into them and cannot surface to breathe, drowning within minutes.

Totoaba Black Market

The totoaba swim bladder fetches up to $50,000 USD per kilogram on the black market in Asia — one of the highest-value wildlife contraband items in the world.

Weak Enforcement

Despite a gillnet ban in the Vaquita refuge zone (established 2015), illegal nets continue to be deployed at night, making enforcement extremely difficult.

Why It Matters

GUARDIAN OF
OCEAN BALANCE

The Vaquita is not merely a beautiful animal — it is an ecological indicator species. Its presence signals a healthy, productive marine ecosystem. Its disappearance would be a warning that the entire food web of the northern Gulf of California is under catastrophic stress.

Controls Fish Populations

Vaquitas feed on a variety of demersal fish, squid, and crustaceans — helping regulate prey populations and preventing overpopulation of certain species.

Supports Marine Food Web

As a mid-trophic predator, the Vaquita bridges prey species and larger predators, maintaining the complex balance of the Gulf's food chains.

Ecosystem Health Indicator

Scientists use Vaquita sightings as a proxy for the overall health of the northern Gulf ecosystem — a living barometer of marine biodiversity.

Biodiversity Anchor

The Gulf of California hosts over 700 fish species, 90 marine mammals, and thousands of invertebrates. Losing the Vaquita disrupts this entire community.

Supports Fishermen's Livelihoods

A healthy Gulf ecosystem sustains billions of dollars in legal fisheries. Conservation directly protects the long-term fishing industry and coastal communities.

Scientific & Cultural Heritage

The Vaquita is a unique evolutionary lineage found nowhere else. Its extinction would be an irreversible loss to science, culture, and all future generations.

When a species disappears, an entire ecosystem changes forever — and so does our understanding of what it means to share this planet.

A Message to Gulf Fishermen

YOU ARE THE KEY
TO SAVING THE VAQUITA

FISHERMEN:
HEROES OF
CONSERVATION

We know that most fishermen in the Gulf of California are not criminals — they are hard-working families who depend on the ocean for their livelihoods. You have fished these waters for generations. That is exactly why your role is so vital: you know the sea better than anyone. You are not the enemy of the Vaquita — you can be its greatest protector.

01

Use Alternative Fishing Gear

Curvina gillnets can be replaced with trawl-excluder devices and alternative nets that do not entangle marine mammals. Government programmes offer subsidies and support.

02

Refuse to Set Illegal Nets

Do not accept payment to set totoaba gillnets. The short-term money comes at the cost of your ocean, your community, and the extinction of a species.

03

Report Illegal Activity

If you see illegal gillnets or poachers operating in the Vaquita refuge, report them anonymously to PROFEPA or CONAPESCA. Your information saves lives.

04

Report Vaquita Sightings

Any confirmed sighting is scientifically invaluable. Note the time, GPS location, and number of animals and report immediately to conservation authorities.

05

Educate Fellow Fishermen

Share what you learn with your crew and community. Awareness on the water is the fastest way to change behaviour and protect the species.

06

Join Vaquita Monitoring Programmes

Paid community monitoring roles are available for local fishermen. Use your sea knowledge to become a guardian — and earn income legitimately.
